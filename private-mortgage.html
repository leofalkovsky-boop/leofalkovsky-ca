Private Mortgage Financing

When a Private Mortgage
Makes Sense

Banks underwrite on rigid rules. Private lenders underwrite on equity. Here's when that difference matters.

01

Bruised or Thin Credit

A past consumer proposal, bankruptcy, or simply not enough credit history shouldn't end your options — private lenders focus on your property's equity, not your score.

02

Self-Employed, Unverifiable Income

If your tax returns don't reflect your real cash flow, private financing bridges the gap while you build a stronger paper trail for an A lender.

03

Bridge Financing

Closing dates don't always line up. A short-term private bridge loan covers the gap between selling one property and closing on the next.

04

Debt Consolidation, Fast

Consolidate high-interest credit cards or CRA debt into a single secured payment — funded in days, not the weeks a bank requires.

05

Avoiding Power of Sale

If you've fallen behind with your current lender, a private mortgage can pay out arrears and buy the time needed to stabilize or sell on your terms.

06

Second Mortgage for Equity

Access equity behind an existing first mortgage you don't want to disturb — for renovations, investment, or a down payment on another property.

Common Questions

Private Mortgage FAQ

What is a private mortgage?
+
A private mortgage is a loan secured against real estate and funded by an individual investor or a Mortgage Investment Corporation (MIC) rather than a bank or credit union. Approval is based primarily on the property's equity and loan-to-value ratio rather than strict income or credit requirements, making it an option when traditional A or B lenders decline an application.
Why would someone need a private mortgage instead of a bank mortgage?
+
Common reasons include being self-employed with unverifiable income, having bruised or thin credit, needing a short-term bridge loan between closings, consolidating high-interest or CRA debt quickly, or avoiding power of sale on an existing property. Private mortgages close faster and with more flexible underwriting than institutional lenders.
What are typical private mortgage rates and terms in Ontario?
+
Private mortgage rates in Ontario typically range from 7–12% annually depending on loan-to-value, property type, and position. First-position private mortgages are generally priced lowest; second-position mortgages carry more risk and higher rates. Terms are usually 1–2 years, interest-only, with lender and broker fees of roughly 1–3% of the loan amount.
How fast can a private mortgage close?
+
Private mortgages can close in as little as 3–10 business days once the property is appraised and legal work is completed, compared to 3–6 weeks for a typical bank-funded mortgage. Speed is one of the main reasons borrowers choose private financing for time-sensitive situations.
Can I use my TFSA to invest in private mortgages in Canada?
+
Yes — a self-directed TFSA can hold private mortgage investments, generating returns of 7–12% completely tax-free. This is one of the most underutilized wealth-building strategies available to Canadians.
What returns can I expect from private mortgage investing in Canada?
+
Private mortgage investments in Canada typically generate 7–12% annually depending on loan-to-value, borrower quality, and position. First-position private mortgages — the safest — typically yield 7–9%. Second-position mortgages carry more risk and yield 10–12%, all secured against real property.
