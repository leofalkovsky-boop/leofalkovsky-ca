What Is the Smith Manoeuvre?

The Smith Manoeuvre is a CRA-approved financial strategy that converts your non-deductible mortgage interest into tax-deductible investment loan interest — allowing Canadians to build wealth while paying down their mortgage.

Developed by Fraser Smith in the 1980s and used by thousands of Canadian homeowners, it requires no extra monthly cash — you simply redirect your existing mortgage payments to do double duty.

Step by Step

How the Smith Manoeuvre Works

The mechanics are straightforward. Each month, as you pay down your mortgage principal, that same amount becomes available to borrow again through a HELOC — and you invest it. The investment interest is then tax-deductible.

1
Restructure Your Mortgage with a Re-advanceable Mortgage
Your mortgage is set up so that as you pay down principal, an equal amount automatically becomes available in a Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC). This is the engine of the Smith Manoeuvre.
2
Borrow from the HELOC to Invest
Each month, you draw from the HELOC the exact amount your mortgage principal decreased and invest it in a diversified, income-producing portfolio. The HELOC balance stays the same; your investment account grows.
3
Deduct the HELOC Interest at Tax Time
Because the borrowed funds are used for investment purposes, the HELOC interest is fully tax-deductible under CRA rules. This converts non-deductible mortgage interest into a legitimate tax deduction.
4
Reinvest Your Annual Tax Refund
Your annual tax refund — generated by the HELOC interest deduction — is applied back to your mortgage principal. This accelerates mortgage payoff and makes even more room in your HELOC for investing.
5
Repeat — Compounding Works in Your Favour
Each year the cycle repeats: more principal paid, more invested, larger tax refund, even more principal paid. Over 25 years, the compounding effect is dramatic — you end up with a paid-off home and a substantial investment portfolio.
